An Empirical Framework for Offline, Privacy-Preserving Browser-Based Inference in Genomic Data Science and Biotechnology Research
Abstract: The increasing demand for genomic data analysis and biotechnology research necessitates the development of secure, offline, and privacy-preserving tools. Cloud-based APIs offer scalable processing, but introduce significant vulnerabilities regarding data leakage and compliance with stringent regulatory frameworks, such as HIPAA and GDPR. This paper presents an open-source, client-side browser architecture powered by localized inference engines, ensuring complete data confidentiality and integrity for sensitive biotechnology research and proprietary datasets.
Introduction: Data security remains a paramount concern in computational biology, where researchers frequently process proprietary genetic sequences, structural biochemical pathways, and unpublished research manuscripts. Relying on centralized cloud APIs exposes high-value intellectual property to external servers, creating compliance bottlenecks. To address this, edge-computing paradigms offer a viable alternative by shifting the computational workload directly to the researcher's local machine. This paper evaluates a system architecture designed to parse browser-level data and route it through a local REST API endpoint for fully containerized, offline analytical processing.
System Architecture & Pipeline: The proposed framework utilizes a modular, three-tier architecture executed entirely within a local client environment:
Client-Side DOM Parsing: A lightweight browser extraction script optimized for specific biological formats (such as FASTA, GenBank, and academic PDFs) to harvest text blocks directly from the active browser viewport.
Local Embedding & Vectorization: A process where parsed biological data is transformed into numerical vectors using a localized, lightweight embedding model, preserving context without external data transmission.
Local Inference Execution: The vectorized data is routed to a local background server running highly optimized, quantized open-weights language models via a loopback address (http://localhost:11434). This allows for context-aware analysis entirely within isolated system memory.
Use Cases in Bioinformatics: This decentralized, local execution framework directly accelerates three critical pillars of life sciences research:
Functional Genomics Screening & Literature Mapping: Researchers can aggregate and synthesize genetic variations from online databases without exposing their specific search criteria or targeted areas of study.
Automated Biochemical Pathway Extraction: The system allows for the immediate extraction of enzymatic relationships and structural pathways directly from open-access journals while keeping the research intent fully confidential.
AI-Assisted Drug Discovery Target Identification: Pre-clinical target validation can be performed locally, eliminating the risk of intellectual property leaks before patent filing.
Metrics & System Evaluation: To assess the feasibility of consumer-grade hardware for this architecture, systems are evaluated on three primary operational metrics:
Token Throughput & Latency: Measuring the time-to-first-token (TTFT) and total tokens per second to ensure that localized, quantized models perform efficiently without dedicated server farms.
Context Window Scalability: Optimizing chunking strategies to handle massive genetic sequences or multi-page manuscripts within standard hardware RAM limitations.
Hardware Resource Footprint: Benchmarking VRAM and CPU utilization to guarantee that background inference does not disrupt concurrent laboratory or computing operations.
